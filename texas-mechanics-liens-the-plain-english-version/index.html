Getting paid for plumbing work is usually straightforward…until it isn’t. Most jobs wrap up, invoices are sent, and payment comes through without a problem. Unfortunately, that’s not always the case. Eventually, nearly every plumber will run into a situation where payment stalls, excuses begin piling up, or a customer suddenly goes quiet.

Situations like this are where a mechanic's lien can become one of the most valuable tools in your toolbox. A mechanic’s lien in Texas is a legal claim against property related to unpaid qualifying work or materials.

This post breaks it down in plain language so you can understand how a mechanic’s lien works, when it applies, and what really matters if you ever need to use it.

What Is a Mechanic’s Lien?

A mechanic's lien is a legal claim placed against a property when someone involved in improving that property hasn't been paid. Think of it like this: You install a new water line, replace a sewer system, or complete a large plumbing project. The work is finished, the customer is happy, but the payment never arrives. A mechanic's lien allows you to attach a claim to the property where the work was performed.

Once the lien has been filed, it becomes part of the public record. Issues like this may delay or complicate selling, refinancing, or transferring the property if the owner needs to complete these transactions. Although the lien doesn't automatically put money in your pocket, it helps give you significant leverage. Many payment disputes are resolved once a property owner realizes a lien has been filed or is about to be filed.

Can Plumbers File a Mechanic's Lien in Texas?

Yes, plumbers can file mechanic's liens in Texas, assuming they meet the legal requirements. This protection isn't limited to large construction companies. Many plumbing professionals may qualify, including independent contractors, plumbing subcontractors, and even suppliers in certain situations.

If you signed a contract directly with the property owner, you may have lien rights as an original contractor. If you're working under a general contractor, you may still have lien rights as a subcontractor, although there are usually additional notice requirements.

The important thing to remember is that lien rights are not automatic in every situation. Texas has many rules, paperwork requirements, and strict deadlines that must be carefully followed. If a critical filing date is missed, you could forfeit your right to enforce a lien.

Constitutional vs. Statutory Liens

Texas is somewhat unique because it recognizes two different types of mechanic's liens, constitutional and statutory.

Constitutional Liens

A constitutional lien comes directly from the Texas Constitution. This type of lien generally applies to contractors who have a direct agreement with the property owner. One of the biggest advantages is that the lien right exists automatically once qualifying work has been performed.

As great as that may sound, constitutional liens have limitations. They don't provide the same level of protection in every situation, and they usually don't extend to subcontractors. Because of these limitations, relying solely on a constitutional lien isn't always the safest approach.

Statutory Liens

Statutory liens are created by Texas property laws. These liens require contractors and subcontractors to follow a series of steps, including notices, documentation, and filing requirements. While the process involves more paperwork, statutory liens often provide stronger and more predictable protection. Most plumbers who deal with payment disputes end up relying on statutory lien procedures.

Common Situations Where Plumbers Use Mechanic's Liens

Most plumbers aren’t thrilled about having to file liens. Still, mechanic's liens are a valuable resource when a customer refuses to pay.

Here are some common situations when plumbing companies use mechanic’s liens:

  • A homeowner refuses to pay the final invoice after work is completed.
  • A general contractor receives payment but never pays the plumbing subcontractor.
  • A commercial project experiences major payment delays.
  • A customer disputes charges without a valid reason.
  • A developer runs into financial trouble before paying contractors.

In many situations, simply sending notice that you intend to file a lien can motivate customers to settle the balance rather quickly. Most property owners try to avoid having liens placed on their home or building, particularly when they’re preparing to sell or refinance soon.

Texas Mechanic’s Lien Requirements for Plumbers

Before filing a lien, plumbers need to make sure they meet several basic requirements first:

#1 Valid Contract or Agreement

A valid agreement can be an important part of establishing a lien claim. Written contracts are the best option because they clearly establish the scope of work, pricing, payment terms, and responsibilities of both parties. While verbal agreements can sometimes create legal rights, they are much harder to prove if a dispute arises.

A simple written contract prior to beginning work can save countless headaches later. Even for smaller jobs, having signed paperwork helps establish exactly what work was agreed upon and how much payment is owed.

#2 Eligible Work and Improvements

Not every plumbing service automatically qualifies for lien protection. Generally speaking, the work must involve improving, repairing, or adding value to the property.

Examples of qualifying plumbing work may include:

  • Installing plumbing systems
  • Replacing water lines
  • Sewer line installations
  • Fixture installation
  • Drainage system work
  • Commercial plumbing construction

Routine service calls may not always fit within the same category as larger improvement projects. That's why understanding the nature of the work performed is crucial before pursuing lien rights.

#3 Proper Documentation

Thorough documentation can be critical when pursuing a lien claim. When payment disputes occur, paperwork can become important evidence.

Keep records such as:

  • Contracts and signed agreements
  • Invoices and payment requests
  • Change orders
  • Photos of completed work
  • Material receipts
  • Emails, texts, and project communications

Many plumbers don't think much about documentation until a dispute arises. By then, trying to gather missing records can become difficult. It’s a good habit to save everything from the beginning of the project.

Notice Requirements for Plumbing Contractors

Texas lien laws include specific notice requirements and deadlines that can catch contractors off guard. In many situations, particularly for subcontractors, required notices must be sent before a lien can be filed. These notices inform the property owner and other parties that payment issues exist.

Different rules may apply to residential and commercial projects. Original contractors and subcontractors can also face different obligations prior to filing a lien. Because these requirements can be highly specific, failing to send a required notice on time can affect a contractor’s lien rights.

Common Mistakes Plumbing Companies Should Avoid

Even the most experienced contractors occasionally make mistakes when dealing with liens.

Here are some of the most common mistakes we see:

  • Waiting too long to take action
  • Missing notice deadlines
  • Failing to maintain proper documentation
  • Filing paperwork with incorrect information
  • Assuming a verbal agreement is enough proof
  • Recording the lien in the wrong county
  • Ignoring legal requirements and procedures

Another mistake is treating mechanic's liens as a last-minute solution. Liens work best when contractors understand the process ahead of time, before a payment dispute occurs. Having a plan in place allows you to move quickly and protect your rights when problems do arise.

Including lien procedures in your standard accounts receivable process can help your business know what steps to take when an invoice remains unpaid. This way, they’ll know exactly what steps to take if an invoice remains unpaid for an extended period.

Bottom Line

A Texas mechanic's lien is one of the most powerful tools plumbers have for protecting themselves against non-payment. It creates a legal claim against a property, giving contractors leverage when large invoices go unpaid.

Texas plumbers may have mechanic’s lien rights when qualifying work goes unpaid, but preserving those rights depends on meeting applicable legal requirements and deadlines. Understanding the process before a payment dispute happens can help plumbers protect their income and improve their chances of getting paid for the work they've already completed.

A mechanic's lien typically attaches to the real estate property where the plumbing work was performed. It creates a hold on the property title, making it difficult for the owner to sell, refinance, or secure financing for the property until your outstanding invoice has been paid and the lien is cleared.

The process typically requires a series of formal documents:

  • Pre-lien monthly notices (especially for commercial or subcontracted work)
  • A formal Notice of Intent to File a Lien
  • The Affidavit of Lien Claim, filed with the county clerk
  • Proof of Service showing the property owner was properly notified

A Notice of Intent to File a Lien is a formal warning letter sent to the property owner stating that you intend to file a mechanic's lien if the unpaid balance is not resolved within a specified number of days. It can be a highly effective tool to prompt immediate payment without having to file the lien.
