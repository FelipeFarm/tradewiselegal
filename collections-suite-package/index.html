The demand letter sequence for unpaid plumbing invoices

What to do when the customer still hasn’t paid

Trying to collect payment is one of the most frustrating parts of running a plumbing business. And while it may be tempting to keep calling, texting, or sending the same invoice again, those informal reminders only get you so far.

A written demand letter creates a record of your collection efforts and lets the customer know you’re serious about getting paid. But one letter isn’t always enough.

Starting friendly and getting firmer at each step gives the customer a chance to make things right while showing that you’re prepared to take the next step if they don’t. If the issue eventually leads to a lien or small claims court, you’ll also have documentation showing the steps you took to collect the debt.

  1. 1

    Friendly 15-day reminder

    Sometimes an unpaid invoice was simply overlooked or forgotten. Start with a courteous reminder that includes the invoice, balance due, past-due date, and payment options. Often, that’s all it takes to get paid without damaging the customer relationship.

  2. 2

    Firm 30-day notice

    Still no payment? It’s time to get more direct. Reference your previous collection attempt, clearly state the amount owed, and give the customer a specific deadline to pay. The goal is to increase the urgency while keeping things professional.

  3. 3

    Final demand before legal action

    If reminders and notices have been ignored, make it clear that the matter is moving to the next level. Give a final payment deadline and let the customer know that additional collection options are being considered if the balance remains unpaid.

  4. 4

    Pre-lien final notice

    If payment still hasn’t come and a mechanic's lien is an option, the final step in the letter sequence is notifying the customer that you intend to file. Texas liens have specific requirements and deadlines, so timing matters.

Get your plumbing invoice paid through a payment plan instead

When a payment plan makes more sense

Not every unpaid invoice involves a customer refusing to pay. Sometimes they intend to pay but simply can’t come up with the full amount right away. In that case, a payment plan may get you paid faster than demanding the entire balance at once.

Put the agreement in writing. It should spell out the payment amounts and due dates, what happens if a payment is missed, and any interest or late fees that apply. A signed agreement gives you something to act on if payments stop, instead of another “pay me when you can” conversation.

When it's time to escalate

But what if they still don’t pay?

  1. 01

    They missed the final deadline

    No response. No payment.

    Your final demand letter has passed its deadline, and the customer still hasn't paid or responded. At this point, sending more reminders probably isn't going to change anything.

  2. 02

    They know they owe you

    They’ve acknowledged the debt.

    The customer agrees the money is owed but continues putting you off, avoiding payment, or making promises that never turn into a check. It may be time to move beyond letters.

  3. 03

    Time is running out

    Lien deadlines are getting close.

    If the unpaid work is tied to a specific property, waiting too long could affect your lien options. Texas lien deadlines matter, so this is one situation you don't want to let drag on.

What is the next step if sending a demand letter isn't enough?

  1. 1

    Check your lien options

    For property-related plumbing work, a mechanic's lien may be your strongest next step.

    A mechanic's lien creates a claim against the property itself, putting pressure on the customer that another letter may not. But Texas liens have strict filing requirements and deadlines, so you need to determine whether a lien is available before you run out of time to use it.

  2. 2

    Consider small claims court

    When a lien isn’t an option, small claims court may be the better path.

    For smaller unpaid invoices or situations where a mechanic's lien doesn’t apply, small claims court can offer a more practical way to pursue the debt. The process is designed to be faster and less expensive than a full civil lawsuit and can be used by business owners without extensive legal involvement.

  3. 3

    Have your paperwork ready

    Keep your original estimate or service agreement, invoice, change orders, customer emails and texts, and copies of every demand letter you sent. A clear paper trail can help support your position, whether you pursue a lien, go to small claims court, or simply need more leverage to get the customer to pay.

    Whichever route you take, documentation matters.
